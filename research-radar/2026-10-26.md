# ai-ethics-lab research radar - week of 2026-10-26

A short, honest look across arXiv, GitHub, Hacker News and the practitioner
press at what the field is actually asking for that this lab can speak to.
"Belongs elsewhere" entries flag sibling labs; this lab builds only what sits
inside its charter.

## What the frontier is pulling toward

### 1. Training-corpus provenance has moved from "nice to have" to a regulatory requirement

The EU AI Act's General-Purpose-AI obligations around training-content summary
and a copyright-compliance policy entered application in August 2025. The
practitioner press through October 2026 is converging on a "Data BOM" pattern:
every corpus declares its source components, their licences and their
transformations, hashed and signed, with a chain of custody that a regulator
or a plaintiff can read. Primary signals this week:

- Industry roundups: *The Top 10 LLM Training Datasets for 2026* (ODS /
  iMerit) note that **"publicly visible" never means "licensed to train,"**
  hubs like Hugging Face and Kaggle carry no license clearance, and corpora
  like Dolma pin only to ODC-BY while leaving constituent-source licences to
  the user to track - the gap is in the manifest layer, not the data.
- Research survey: *Data Lineage for AI Systems* (TianPan, 2026-04-20)
  argues provenance has to be built into the pipeline, not reconstructed
  after a regulator asks.
- Patterns: *10 Data Provenance Patterns to Prove AI Training Sources
  On-Chain* (2026-xx) and *Legal and Ethical Guide 2026* (DataSos) both
  land on the same core: source, licence, transformation, hashed, signed,
  anchored.

**What the lab can speak to.** Compass primitive **E5** states this rule
directly: every document carries its testimony class and chain of custody;
machine-made text is its own class; independent testifiers are counted once.
The implementation gap is at the manifest - the lab's `corpuslint` fills it.

### 2. System-prompt safety is being formalised as a benchmark, not a claim

The MLCommons Jailbreak Benchmark v1.0 landed in October 2026 (arXiv
2610.02827). It evaluates eight open-weight systems against 264 seed prompts
across eleven hazard categories; the first substantive public finding is that
representative adversarial prompting *substantially* weakens safeguards that
hold on unmodified prompts. Multi-turn benchmarks (MultiBreak, Teleai-Safety)
and intent-oriented systematisations (SoK: Multi-Turn LLM Jailbreaks) are
moving the field from "we tested" to "we tested *this* set, versioned."

**What the lab can speak to.** The compass ships a system prompt and names
five probes a model should answer differently if the prompt took. Shipping
the probes as a versioned, scorable set is the right move at the right time;
`probeset` does that. It is not a jailbreak benchmark - it is the prompt's
own promise, made falsifiable.

### 3. "Red-teaming" the discipline is still fighting the vocabulary

CSET's October 2026 note (*What Does AI Red-Teaming Actually Mean?*) and
industry 2026 guides (Mindgard, StingrAI, DataVLab) all land on the same
observation: red-teaming, adversarial testing and AI pen-testing are used
interchangeably in buyer and vendor language; Microsoft's AI Red Team splits
*safety* from *security* and the industry still blurs them. The money is
moving (the services market grew ~29% year-on-year to USD 2.26B in 2026),
but the words have not settled.

**What the lab can speak to.** Compass primitive **G1** (dialectical
red-team on contested terms) is directly on this problem. The candidate-pool
item `redteam-dialectic` is a natural next build - deferred this week to
keep the batch focused and world-class.

### 4. Multi-turn jailbreak taxonomies and personas-as-attack

The strongest arXiv work this month (*How Jailbreak Attacks Inform Safety
Alignment*, *Enhancing Jailbreak Attacks on LLMs via Persona Prompts*)
treats the model-as-defender problem as a game whose structure is persona-
and role-play-shaped. This is defensive-security territory; the compass
touches it in E7 and C3 (self-descriptions as model) but a lab-level product
is **outside this lab's charter**. Belongs elsewhere.

**Belongs elsewhere.** Agent-level runtime containment: **agent-guard-lab**.
Red-team-service infrastructure: **governance-lab** or **defense-lab**.

### 5. Dataset-card / data-sheet tooling has fragmented, not consolidated

Hugging Face's dataset-card template, the Datasheets-for-Datasets paper
template, and vendor-specific data-governance tooling (Collibra, Alation,
Monte Carlo for data lineage) do not interoperate. The practitioner press
converged this quarter on the Data BOM pattern as the lingua franca across
them; nobody has shipped a free, licence-permissive linter that enforces it
at the manifest layer. The gap is small, concrete, and current.

**What the lab can speak to.** This is the lane `corpuslint` opens into.

### 6. Agentic jailbreak and tool-use red-teaming is still unmet

*SoK: Rethinking Jailbreaking in the Era of Agentic AI* (arXiv 2609.12413)
calls for defender-side evaluation of agent runtimes, not just chat
responses. The compass touches this in B1 (instruction parsing), D4
(authority) and E4 (ledgers) but the primary lane is runtime containment -
**agent-guard-lab**.

**Belongs elsewhere.** agent-guard-lab.

### 7. The International AI Safety Report 2026 (arXiv 2602.21012)

A survey document, not a tool. Worth reading for the risk-class vocabulary
it is converging on. Belongs nowhere in particular; influence on charters
is diffuse.

### 8. Provenance watermarking (Ward: Provable RAG Dataset Inference via LLM Watermarks)

A model-level attestation approach - complementary to a manifest-level
check, not a replacement. `corpuslint` and watermark-based attestation
should coexist: the former checks the claim before the fact, the latter
after. Noting for future-work; the manifest is the simpler, broader win.

### 9. GPT-NL Public Corpus (arXiv 2604.00920)

An example of what a permissively-licensed, Dutch-first corpus looks like
when the Data BOM is published. A test input for `corpuslint` to validate
against in a later release.

### 10. The 2024 Foundation Model Transparency Index (arXiv 2407.12929)

Measures transparency at the model level, not the corpus. Scope-adjacent;
corpus-level discipline is a precondition for the index's data-practice
columns to even be scorable.

## What we chose to build this week, and why

Two artifacts enter the batch. Both anchor to compass primitives already
present in `compass/compass.json`; both are standard-library-only; both are
plain-ASCII, test-first, Apache-2.0, and under the lab's shared CLI
contract.

### 1. `corpuslint` - a linter for corpus manifests (E5)

- **Why now.** Signals 1 and 5 point at the same gap at the manifest
  layer. The regulatory need is current; the industry pattern is settling;
  no free, permissively-licensed, standard-library-only linter enforces it.
  The compass states the rule up front and names its falsifier; the gate it
  defines fits at the manifest, which is cheap and binary.
- **What it is.** `python -m corpuslint lint manifest.json`. Thirteen
  rules (CORP-001..013) with stable ids: testimony-class typing, chain-of-
  custody shape and ordering, machine-generated detection, inflated-
  independence accounting, origin shape, manifest-level signals. HIGH /
  MEDIUM / INFO severity ladder; healthy / needs-attention / unhealthy /
  unknown verdict rollup.
- **Honest limits.** It is structural, not content-level; it does not call
  the network, does not verify signatures, does not score testifiers. It
  makes false claims about a corpus expensive to make.

### 2. `probeset` - a scorable set of the compass system-prompt probes

- **Why now.** Signal 2. The compass ships a system prompt and claims
  five probes test whether it took - but the probes are prose. The field
  is moving to versioned, scorable benchmarks; the compass should keep up
  with its own promise.
- **What it is.** Five probes as data (`probeset/data/probes.json`), each
  with id, compass-rule mapping, prompt, expectation, required markers,
  must-not markers. A deterministic rule-based evaluator reads a model's
  responses (pasted by the user) and produces a per-probe pass/fail with a
  verdict rollup. The harness calls no model.
- **Honest limits.** Marker matching. A model that uses other vocabulary
  for the right answer fails; a model that recites marker words without
  the discipline passes. The point is to make the compass's claim cheap
  to check; a human reviewer should spot-check borderline cases.

### What we deliberately did not build

- `redteam-dialectic` (G1, Signal 3). Strong frontier fit, but a world-
  class first cut needs a dialectic-schema and worked examples that would
  push this week's batch past the test-first bar. Hold for 2026-11-02.
- `dutyledger` (E3). Interesting but no urgent frontier pull this week.
- `boundarycard` (A5). Same.
- `eventtypes` (C1). Same.
